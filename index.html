Why June?

Mid-to-late June is the sweet spot: long daylight (sunset around 10 pm!), the lakes have thawed to their famous turquoise, the Moraine Lake road and shuttles are running, and it's before the peak July–August crowds. Early June can still have ice on the higher lakes and snow on trails, so we're aiming for roughly June 14–30.

Bonus: Calgary is on Mountain Time, only 1 hour ahead of San Diego, so no jet lag.

DAY 1 ✈️ Travel day

San Diego → Calgary → Banff

Sample flight shape (NOT a real booking or schedule):
🛫 ~8:00 AM leave San Diego → connect in Seattle, Denver or Vancouver → 🛬 ~3:00 PM arrive Calgary (Mountain Time, 1 hr ahead) → 🚙 pick up SUV ~4 PM → Banff by ~6 PM, in full daylight (sunset is ~10 PM).
  • ✈️ Fly SAN → Calgary (YYC). Usually 1 stop, about 5–6 hours door to gate.
  • 🛂 Entry: US citizens need a passport, but no visa and no eTA. ArriveCAN's Advance Declaration is optional (can save time at the airport kiosks).
  • 🧥 Keep a fleece in the carry-on: Calgary can be in the 50s °F when we land.
  • 🚙 Pick up the rental SUV and drive ~1.5 hrs west; the mountains suddenly appear out of the prairie.
  • 🛍️ Evening stroll on Banff Avenue: shops, ice cream, BeaverTails, mountains at the end of every street.
  • 🦌 Keep eyes open for elk wandering right through town.
🎫 Buy the Parks Canada Discovery Pass (family/group, about CA$167.50 at 2026 prices; we'd estimated ~CA$151) online before we go or at the park gate. Kids 17 and under get in free anyway.
DAY 2 😴 SLEEP-IN

Banff: Gondola, hot springs & sunset lakes

  • 🚠 Ride the Banff Gondola up Sulphur Mountain (2,281 m / 7,486 ft). Walk the summit boardwalk to the old weather station, with views over six mountain ranges.
  • ♨️ Soak at the Banff Upper Hot Springs, a natural hot pool with a mountain view (about CA$64 for a family, 2026 price).
  • 🛶 Late afternoon: paddle a canoe on the Bow River / Vermilion Lakes (rentals from the Banff Canoe Club) or just drive Vermilion Lakes Rd for the famous Mount Rundle sunset reflection.
💡 Book the gondola for early morning or evening. Midday lines are long.
DAY 3 ⏰ EARLY START (~7 AM)

Johnston Canyon & wildlife drive

  • 🥾 Johnston Canyon: steel catwalks bolted to the canyon walls, right above rushing water. Lower Falls is ~1.1 km (easy) and has a little cave you can walk through to get spray in your face; Upper Falls is ~2.6 km one-way.
  • 🦌 Drive the Bow Valley Parkway slowly, one of the best places to spot elk, bighorn sheep, deer and sometimes bears.
  • 🌊 Optional: Lake Minnewanka boat cruise or skip-stones at Two Jack Lake.
⏰ Get to Johnston Canyon by ~8 am. By 10 the parking lot is full and the catwalks are crowded.
DAY 4 ⏰ EARLY START (~6:30–7 AM)

Lake Louise & the Tea House hike

  • 🚙 Leave Banff by ~7 AM (45 min). Lake Louise's lakeshore lot fills very early, so park at the Park & Ride and take the Parks Canada shuttle, or arrive before ~7:30 AM and pay to park (about CA$42/day).
  • 🛶 Rent a red canoe on Lake Louise (pricey, well over CA$100 for a short paddle, estimate) or walk the lakeshore trail.
  • 🫖 Hike to the Lake Agnes Tea House: ~3.5 km up (~400 m climb) to a log teahouse (first opened in 1901) beside a hidden lake. Tea, soup and biscuits at the top. Bring cash just in case.
  • 🏨 Afternoon: check in at Lake Louise, hot tub, early night (tomorrow is the sunrise day).
🎟️ Shuttle seats go fast. In 2026, 40% of Moraine/Lake Louise shuttle seats opened in spring (April 15) and the rest 2 days before each date. Plan to book the day they open.
DAY 5 ⏰ EARLIEST START (~6 AM)

Moraine Lake sunrise, then Yoho

  • 🌅 Moraine Lake: the "Valley of the Ten Peaks". Take an early shuttle (Parks Canada, a commercial shuttle, or the Lake Louise Inn's summer shuttle if we stay there), since no private cars are allowed on the Moraine Lake road. Climb the Rockpile for the photo.
  • ☕ Back to Lake Louise village for a big breakfast (Laggan's), then a short rest.
  • 💚 Afternoon in Yoho National Park (BC, still on Mountain Time): Natural Bridge and Emerald Lake (canoes, easy 5 km loop).
  • 💦 Optional: Takakkaw Falls. The road has tight switchbacks, so skip it if anyone gets carsick.
DAY 6 ⏰ EARLY START (~7 AM) · long day

The Icefields Parkway (big day!)

  • 🏞️ Bow Lake: mirror reflections. Then Peyto Lake, the wolf-head-shaped turquoise lake (short walk to the viewpoint).
  • 🧊 Columbia Icefield Adventure: ride a giant six-wheeled Ice Explorer onto the Athabasca Glacier, walk on 10,000-year-old ice, and fill a bottle with glacier water. Then the Skywalk, a glass-floored walkway 280 m above the valley. (~2.5–3 hr tour; 2027 dates May 1–Oct 11, weather dependent.)
  • 💦 Athabasca Falls on the way into Jasper.
Planned stops (every ~30–45 min, drive times approximate):
Lake Louise → Bow Lake (~30 min) → Peyto Lake (~10 min) → Mistaya Canyon / Saskatchewan Crossing (~30 min: gas, restrooms, snacks) → Weeping Wall / Big Bend viewpoint (~30 min) → Columbia Icefield Discovery Centre (~15 min: tour, lunch, real restrooms) → Sunwapta Falls (~40 min) → Athabasca Falls (~20 min) → Jasper (~25 min). About 3.5 hrs of driving spread over a full day.
⛽ Fill the tank in Lake Louise. Saskatchewan Crossing (The Crossing Resort) is the only fuel stop on the Parkway, seasonal (about May–Oct) and pricey, so call ahead. Download offline maps: most of the Parkway has no cell service.
DAY 7 Moderate start (~8 AM)

Jasper: Maligne Lake & Spirit Island

  • 🚤 Maligne Lake Cruise (~1.5 hrs) 14 km down the lake to Spirit Island, one of the most photographed places in Canada. You can only see it by boat.
  • 🫎 Maligne Lake Road is moose, bear and caribou country. Drive slowly at dawn and dusk. Stop at Medicine Lake (it "disappears" each fall).
  • ✨ After dark: stargazing. Jasper is one of the world's largest Dark Sky Preserves.
⚠️ Maligne Canyon is closed through the 2026 season because of damage from the 2024 Jasper wildfire, with no reopening date yet. If it's still closed in 2027 we swap in Athabasca Falls / Sunwapta Falls or the Moose Lake Loop.
DAY 8 😴 SLEEP-IN

Jasper SkyTram, lakes & town

  • 🚡 Jasper SkyTram: a 7-minute ride up Whistlers Mountain to 2,263 m (7,425 ft). Hike to the summit if there's no snow; it's about 12 °C (20 °F) colder up top than in town!
  • 🚲 Afternoon: rent bikes or paddleboards at Pyramid Lake / Patricia Lake, or float the Athabasca River.
  • 🍕 Dinner in town, pick up souvenirs, then one last stargazing session.
🔥 Jasper is still rebuilding after the July 2024 wildfire. The town, hotels and nearly all trails are open, but lodging is tighter, so book early.
DAY 9 ✈️ Travel day

Heading home

  • Option A: drive back to Calgary (~4.5–5 hrs) down the Icefields Parkway, a second chance at every viewpoint, then fly YYC → SAN.
  • Option B: drive east to Edmonton (~4 hrs) and fly YEG → SAN. Less scenic, but sometimes cheaper, and it can save a long day. (One-way car rentals often have a drop fee.)
✈️ Book an afternoon/evening flight so we're not racing the clock.

Car time

Road-trip survival

🤢 Motion sickness

Most roads are gentle, but Maligne Lake Rd, the Big Bend hill and the Yoho Valley switchbacks are curvy. Whoever gets queasy rides up front and watches the horizon. No reading or scrolling on twisty bits, keep fresh air coming, and bring ginger chews or candy. Ask a pharmacist about Dramamine/Bonine or Sea-Bands (try them at home first). Stopping every 30–45 min helps a lot.

⛽ The gas gap

Lake Louise → Jasper is ~230 km with one seasonal fuel stop, at Saskatchewan Crossing (The Crossing Resort, about May–Oct; call ahead, high prices). Fill up in Lake Louise. EV charging there is limited.

🧑‍✈️ Who drives

Jason is the main driver, with Akiko added as the second driver. Policies we checked: Enterprise and Avis Canada list a spouse as a free additional driver (Avis: 25+ with a valid license); Hertz charges unless you're a Gold Plus Rewards member. The spouse shows their license at pickup, and US licenses are fine in Alberta. Confirm with whichever company we book.

Weather

Is it cold? (A little!)

June days are usually sunny and in the 60s–70s °F, but mountain mornings are crisp (often 30s–40s °F), and it gets much colder up on the gondola, the SkyTram and the glacier. Afternoon showers are common, and snow is possible at higher elevations even in June.

Stay safe

Mountain safety, the short version

🐻

Bears & bear spray

Grizzlies and black bears live here. On trails: carry bear spray (know how to use it, video below), hike together, and talk or make noise. Never leave food in the open. You can't fly with bear spray, so buy it in Calgary or Banff (or rent in Banff).

🦌

Give wildlife space

Parks Canada says stay 100 m (10 bus lengths) from bears, wolves and cougars, and 30 m (3 bus lengths) from elk, sheep, deer and moose. Elk can charge in calving season (late May–June). Stay in the car at "bear jams", and never feed animals.

🧊

Glaciers & crevasses

Only walk on the glacier inside the marked safe area of the Ice Explorer tour. Hidden crevasses on the Athabasca toe have killed people. Don't climb past the signs.

🥶

Cold water

Glacier lakes and rivers are only a few degrees above freezing, so falling in can cause cold shock fast. Always wear the life jacket in canoes, stay seated, and keep away from riverbanks and waterfall edges.

⛰️

Altitude

Banff is about 1,380 m (4,500 ft) and the gondola/SkyTram tops are ~2,270 m (7,450 ft). That's mild, but you may feel short of breath. Drink extra water, take it easy the first day, and wear sunscreen.

⛈️

Weather & roads

Weather changes quickly: carry rain gear and layers. Check road conditions on 511 Alberta. Fill up on gas before the Icefields Parkway, and don't stop in traffic lanes for photos.

Will we see bears?

Maybe! Bears are most often seen from the car along roadsides in June, especially on the Bow Valley Parkway, Icefields Parkway and Maligne Lake Road. Seeing one from a safe distance in the car is the best way. Elk and bighorn sheep are almost guaranteed.

🛡️ Travel insurance: worth a look once we book. Get a policy covering trip cancellation plus emergency medical, since US health plans often cover little or nothing in Canada.

💳 Cards

Visa and Mastercard work almost everywhere, and tap-to-pay is standard. Use a card with no foreign transaction fee. Amex is accepted less often. Carry a little CA$ cash for small shops and the tea house.

💵 Tipping

Like the US: 15–20% at sit-down restaurants, a few dollars for tour guides and housekeeping. Card machines will prompt you.

🧾 Taxes

Alberta adds 5% GST (no provincial sales tax). BC (Yoho) adds GST + 7% PST. Hotels add tourism levies.

Passports

Everyone needs a passport book

Flying into Canada needs a passport book for each of us. A passport card only works by land or sea. US citizens don't need a visa or eTA.

Kate & Emily (under 16): first-time and renewal passports must be applied for in person with Form DS-11, and both parents should come along with the girls. If one parent can't, they sign a notarized Form DS-3053 consent. You need proof of citizenship (birth certificate), parent IDs and a photo. Child passports are valid 5 years. Fees: $100 application + $35 acceptance each (2026).

Timeline (State Dept., Oct 2026): routine processing 4–6 weeks, expedited 2–3 weeks (+$60), plus up to 2 weeks mailing each way. The State Department says the quietest time to apply is October–December, so applying this winter is perfect.

Jason & Akiko: check your passport expiry dates now. If either expires before early 2028, renew early.

Note: if either girl turns 16 before applying, they still apply in person with DS-11 for a first passport, and a parent must be aware of the application. Source: travel.state.gov.

Questions

FAQ

Is it going to be cold?

Days are usually pleasant (60s–70s °F), but mornings are chilly (30s–40s) and it's colder and windy on the glacier and mountaintops. Bring a warm jacket, beanie and rain jacket and you'll be comfy.

Will we see bears?

There's a decent chance in June, usually from the car along the roadsides. Elk, bighorn sheep and deer are almost guaranteed; moose are possible around Jasper. We always keep our distance and carry bear spray on hikes.

How much driving is it?

About 900–1,000 km total. Most days have only 30 min–2 hrs of driving. Day 6 (Icefields Parkway) and the last day are the long ones, but the Parkway is the attraction, with stops every 20 minutes.

Will there be Wi-Fi?

Yes at hotels and cafés in town. No cell service on most of the Icefields Parkway and on many trails, so download stuff in advance.

Is there shopping?

Yes! Banff Avenue is packed with outdoor brands, souvenir and Canadian shops, and treats. Jasper and Lake Louise have smaller shops, and Calgary has big malls if we have time on the way in or out.

What if it rains?

Mountain showers usually pass quickly. Rainy-day ideas: the hot springs (fun in the rain!), Cave and Basin National Historic Site, the Whyte Museum and Banff Park Museum, shopping on Banff Ave, a lake cruise, or a cozy café. Waterfalls are even bigger after rain.

Can we swim?

The lakes are glacier-fed and freezing, so it's a quick polar-plunge dare at most. The Banff Upper Hot Springs (warm!) and hotel pools are the real swimming. Bring swimsuits.

Will we have jet lag?

Nope. Alberta is on Mountain Time, just 1 hour ahead of San Diego. The bigger adjustment is that it stays light until about 10 pm, so bring a sleep mask.

How long is the flight?

About 5–6 hours including one connection (often Seattle, Denver or Vancouver). We'll also check whether any seasonal nonstop is offered when we book.

Do the hikes need to be hard?

No. Everything in the plan has an easy option: Johnston Canyon Lower Falls, Lake Louise shoreline, Peyto viewpoint, Emerald Lake loop. Lake Agnes is the "bigger" hike (~2.5–3 hrs round trip) with a teahouse reward.

Is anything booked?

No, this is a draft idea. All prices are estimates. If we decide to go: Jasper lodging by ~Dec 2026, Banff and Lake Louise soon after, passports this winter, and Moraine shuttle seats when they open in spring 2027.

Do we need an eTA or ArriveCAN?

No eTA or visa for US citizens, just passports. ArriveCAN's Advance Declaration is optional; you can fill it out up to 72 hours before landing to speed up the customs kiosks.
